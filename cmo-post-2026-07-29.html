Five stars said nothing. Two sentences said everything. I sorted 346 reviews this month. Restaurants near me, real guests, real words, not survey data. Most owners check the star average and stop there. Four point eight, four point nine, good enough, on to the next thing on the list. I kept reading past the number. Every review got sorted into one of four piles: silent on service, generic, specific, or emotional and named. A five star review with no name, no detail, no moment mentioned, that guest was fine. Fine isn't the same as won over. Another five star, same rating, two rows down. Named the server. Named a dish. Said she'd be back Friday. Same star rating. Two completely different rooms. The rating never told me which restaurant I was actually looking at. The words did. 346 reviews down to one sentence: guests don't care about your star rating. They care about being seen, and they'll tell you straight out when they were, if you read past the number. Go pull your last twenty five stars. Sort them into two piles, named something specific, or didn't. Whichever pile is bigger is the real answer about your restaurant right now. Most owners have never once read their reviews this way. DM me FLOOR and I'll run that sort for you. Free. No charge, no pitch buried in it. P.S. 346 reviews taught me one thing stars never will.
