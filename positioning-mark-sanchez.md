## COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Who else is in this space:**
Generic hospitality consultants, FOH training programs, restaurant coaches, and online courses for servers and managers. At the premium end: large hospitality consulting firms. None of them publish before/after review data as proof of methodology.

**What they offer:**
SOPs, compliance training, customer service frameworks, management systems, and digital courses. Built to scale, not to go deep. Proof is typically testimonials or logos — not public, verifiable business metrics.

**The gap they leave open:**
Nobody in this space has documented proof that their approach produces measurable, transferable results at the floor level. Mark has two restaurants worth of public before/after data, team transfer evidence, guest language shift analysis, and departure impact measurement. That category of proof does not exist elsewhere in hospitality consulting. That is the white space Unforgettable Hospitality owns.

---

## STRATEGIC ANCHORS

**Frame of Reference:** Measured guest experience transfer

**Point of Difference (POD):**
Two datasets. Public. Verifiable. Booty's Wings: 103 named five-star reviews in one quarter, 92% five-star rate, 9x jump in negative reviews after departure. Zeta's Grill: 5-star review pace from 5/month to 58/month, 108 personally generated reviews in 12 weeks, team generating 36 more without formal training, review pace dropping 86% during a vacation and jumping 598% on return — but the standard not disappearing. No competitor in this space has anything close to this level of documented, public proof. The methodology is not claimed — it is evidenced.

**Point of Parity (POP):**
Must demonstrate 20+ years of real restaurant experience, the ability to accurately diagnose FOH breakdowns, and the capacity to build training and systems that produce visible results.

**Strategic Sacrifice:**
Not for owners who want a quick fix or a template. Not for large chains seeking scalable, system-only solutions that bypass the human layer. Not for anyone who wants results without presence — the model requires being in the room. And not for clients who won't allow the results to be tracked — the methodology depends on measurement.

## STRATEGIC DO'S AND DON'TS

**DO:**
1. Lead with the data in business-to-business contexts — two restaurants, documented before/after, team transfer evidence, departure impact. No competitor can match it.
2. Let the Booty's departure story do heavy lifting for the dependency argument — 92% to 59%, negative reviews 9x, documented by public reviews the owner can look up themselves.
3. Use the Zeta's before/after for the growth argument — 5/month to 58/month, guest language shift documented, team generating results before formal training began.
4. Use the vacation data for the transfer argument — the standard slowed but didn't die while Mark was absent. That's the proof of concept for what a full engagement delivers.
5. Always speak to the three client types separately in content — their problems, proof requirements, and emotional triggers are different enough to require distinct messaging.

**DON'T:**
1. Lead with "consultant" — it's the wrong frame and undersells the work. Lead with the result, then explain the method.
2. Aggregate all the stats into one post or pitch — one data point per conversation, one stat per post. Let each one land before introducing the next.
3. Try to serve large chains that want scalable, system-only solutions — the model requires presence and depth, which doesn't scale at volume without changing what the work is.
4. Use generic hospitality language ("elevating the guest experience," "creating memorable moments") without the data attached — it sounds like every other consultant in the space.
5. Pitch before the results are visible — the model of walking in and proving value before asking for the contract is a structural competitive advantage.
