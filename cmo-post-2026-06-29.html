Hook (highlighted)

I thought I was just good at my job.

The reviews proved I was a liability.

In my final quarter at Booty's Wings, guests named me in 103 five-star reviews. I was mentioned 20x more than the next closest person on staff. That felt like a win. It wasn't. Three months after I left, the five-star rate dropped from 92% to 59%. Negative reviews jumped from 2.9% to 26.6%. A 9x increase. Publicly. In the Google reviews anyone can look up right now. The owner didn't have a staffing problem after I left. He had a standards problem the whole time I was there. Here's what nobody in this industry says out loud: one strong performer isn't proof your restaurant works. It's proof your restaurant depends on that one person. And when they leave, the data tells the truth immediately. I'm not a unicorn. I tracked what I did. I documented it. Then I took the same approach to a second restaurant and the team started generating reviews before formal training ever started. That's not talent. That's a system. And systems can be built. If your guest experience walks out the door every time the right person ends their shift, that's not a hiring problem. That's a standards problem. And it's sitting right now in your public review history. If your best server left tomorrow, what would your review rate look like in 90 days?
