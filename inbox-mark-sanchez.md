## SECTION 3: HOW DELIVERY WORKS

For restaurant owners/groups:
1. Mark walks into the restaurant — often by working a shift before any formal engagement is discussed
2. Conducts floor-level audit: observes team, interacts with guests, identifies real breakdown points, reviews public review history for volume/rate/language data
3. Models the standard on the floor before formal training begins — team transfer begins through observation
4. Builds systems, templates, training resources, and communication standards specific to that restaurant
5. Trains staff and leadership directly on the floor, in real time
6. Installs emotional intelligence practices alongside operational systems
7. Tracks results via review data and guest language shifts throughout the engagement

For individual professionals (Tips to Freedom):
1. Initial conversation to identify biggest issue (money, emotional regulation, floor presence)
2. Four weeks of one-on-one coaching
3. Focus on practical floor skills, emotional boundaries, and income strategy

## SECTION 9: COMMUNICATION GUIDELINES

**Tone by context:**
- LinkedIn (owners/groups): Data-backed authority. Lead with a specific stat or observation, follow with the story behind it. Never generic hospitality advice.
- LinkedIn (professionals): Personal transformation + proof of what's possible. "Named in 103 reviews" lands differently for a server than for an owner.
- Sales conversations: Proof before pitch. Let the data speak first. Walk in before asking for the contract.
- Client onboarding: Direct, warm, clear. Set expectations for presence, floor time, and what gets measured.

**Language to use:**
measurable · transferable · mirrored · guest language · before/after · named reviews · five-star rate · on the floor · team transfer · in the trenches · standard · presence · be about it · documented · felt the experience · the data

**Language to avoid:**
optimize · leverage · best practices · scalable · holistic · pain points · passionate (without proof) · world-class · curated experience · elevate

**Hard limits:**
Never position as an outside advisor. Never use aggregate stats without clear context. Never claim results without the documented data to support them.
